What you are looking at

The top row is the decoded picture, zoomed 4× with no smoothing. The bottom row is CIEDE2000 error against the source for the same pixels — black is no colour error, blue about 1, magenta about 2.5, white 5 and up. A dE00 of 1 is roughly the threshold where a trained eye starts to notice on a flat field.

The crop is not the whole frame, and that is the point. Averaged over all of bbb_1080p.png the three encodes read … — which sounds like a rounding error. Chroma subsampling does not spread its damage evenly: it sits on saturated colour edges and costs nothing anywhere else. This window was picked by maximising dE00(4:2:0) − dE00(4:4:4), so it lands where subsampling hurts rather than where quantisation does.

Where to look, specifically

GNC upsamples chroma with nearest neighbour — chroma_upsample.wgsl is essentially src[(dy>>shift_y) * src_width + (dx>>shift_x)]. So the artefact is blocky, not blurry: colour is constant across 2×1 blocks at 4:2:2 and 2×2 blocks at 4:2:0. Once you know to look for a staircase rather than a smudge it is hard to unsee.

  • 4:4:4 → 4:2:2 loses horizontal chroma only. Look at vertical colour edges.
  • 4:2:2 → 4:2:0 loses vertical chroma as well. Look at horizontal colour edges — the top and bottom of a coloured object.
  • Best targets are saturated colour against neutral, and thin coloured detail.

Two traps

Luma barely moves. In YCoCg-R — the plane GNC actually codes — luma runs 52.95 → 52.51 → 52.33 dB across the three formats at q=95. The damage is entirely colour. But luma computed from decoded RGB reads 54.34 → 48.07 → 46.04 dB, which looks like an 8 dB luma collapse. It is contamination: perturb only Co/Cg and the reconstructed RGB moves, so BT.709 luma moves with it.

VMAF cannot see any of this. It scores the luma plane only. On a chroma question your eye is a better instrument than VMAF, and dE00 is better than both.
